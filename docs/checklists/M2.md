# M2 in-game check: the Threat Monitor

The offline tests prove that the code agrees with our assumptions about the client; this checklist proves that the
client agrees with the code. It checks the exit criterion of [milestone M2](../roadmap/M2-threat-monitor.md) (*in a
party, the list shows the tank and every member's percentage on the target, a DPS closing on the tank goes WARNING then
DANGER, and a target with no data shows UNKNOWN with its reason*) and it is **not done yet**: nobody has run the addon
on WoW Forever. The monitor was written before the [M0 probe](../spike/README.md) was run, on assumptions.

**Run the probe first, or together.** Its results say which threat data the client gives an addon (P1–P4), which
units answer and how the events behave (P5, P6, P9) and what sampling costs (P10); M0.7 will revise M2 with them. Start
with the probe's [procedure per context](../spike/README.md#in-game-procedure-per-context) and run this checklist in
the same sessions: the same party, dungeon and raid serve both. For the CPU measurements (step 10) use one tool at a
time: `/atwp watch off` and no `/atwp perf` run while you measure the monitor, and hide the monitor window while
`/atwp perf` runs.

Report the results with the [*In-game test report* form](https://github.com/Finland6738/azeroth-threatwarden/issues/new?template=ingame_test.yml)
(*Check*: for example `M2 step 4.3`) and note them in the table [at the end](#result). One report for everything that
passed is enough; open one per thing that did not.

**A "fail" is not always a bug.** If the client hides the threat values, *state only* or *unavailable* is the right
answer: write down what you saw. What must never happen is an invented number, a 0 % standing for "unknown", a state
without its icon and word, or a Lua error.

## What you need

- WoW Forever, a character for the solo checks, and an enemy with many hit points (an elite) or a training dummy.
- A **party** with a tank and at least one damage dealer (you are one of them; swap roles if you can), a **dungeon**
  and, if you can, a **raid**, ideally 40 players.
- The addon with the monitor switched on: `v0.2.0` or later, or a development copy of an up-to-date `main`.
  `/atw debug` must say `on: monitor` in *Features*.
- On the PC: Python 3, the repository on an up-to-date `main` and `tools/local.ini` with your AddOns folder, as in the
  [M1 checklist](M1.md#what-you-need):

```bash
python -I -X utf8 tools/fetch_libs.py
```

```bash
python -I -X utf8 tools/install_addon.py
```

  Restart the game after installing. The probe is installed with `python -I -X utf8 tools/install_probe.py`.
- Lua errors visible: `/console scriptErrors 1`, or BugSack.
- Run the [M1 checklist](M1.md) first if you never did: the window and the settings are assumed to work.

`/atw debug` has no player, guild or realm names, so its text is safe to paste. Screenshots of the monitor show names:
replace them with `<player>`.

## 1. Before the fight

1. Log in: no Lua error, no chat message. The Monitor tab no longer says that monitoring arrives later; with no enemy
   targeted it shows **No target** and *Target an enemy to see its threat*; the title bar pill says **Idle**.
2. Target a hostile mob without attacking it: its name (and *Elite*, *Rare*, *Rare elite* or *Boss* if it is one),
   **Not in combat**, no numbers. Note any mob whose name reads *Unknown mob* or whose kind is wrong.
3. Target yourself, a friendly NPC, a corpse: *No target* (a friend that targets an enemy is step 3.4).
4. `/atw debug`: the sections *Features*, *Target*, *Threat scanner* (`idle`) and *Scheduler* (`no tick running`) are
   there and nothing is under *Last errors*. Copy the report.

## 2. Solo, on your target

1. Pull the mob. Within a second the pill says **Live**, **State only** or **Unavailable**: write down which. The
   window shows by itself if you left the default (step 8.5).
2. In **Live**: your row (ringed) shows class colour, bar, percentage (100 % while you hold the mob), state icon and
   word; the header shows **Tank:** with your name and the shield on your row. You hold it alone: your state is
   **Safe** with *No one else on the threat list* in the tooltip, not Danger.
3. With a **pet**: the pet has its own row. Let the pet hold the mob and pull it off: the tank line follows, and your
   state goes Warning and Danger as you close.
4. Hover your row: every value has its kind (*detected*, *calculated*, *estimated*, *predicted*) and every missing one
   its reason. Compare the percentage with the game's own threat display (if it has one) and write down any difference.
5. The footer says *Updated 0.x s ago · N % valid (10 s)*: the age stays small in combat; note the percentage.
6. `/atw debug` **during the fight**: *Threat scanner* `active`, the mode and its reason, and the *Capabilities* lines
   (`threatValues`, `threatState`, `threatLead`, `threatEvents`, `mobIdentity`). Copy it: it is the main result.

## 3. Party: the tank line, the margin and the states

The tank pulls a mob with many hit points. The tank has the role **Tank** assigned; do one run without roles too.

1. Every member is listed (pets of others are not), with class colour, role icon, and the shield on the tank. The
   header says **Tank: name**. A member who has not done anything to the mob has no row.
2. **Tank's view** (look at the tank's screen, or be the tank): the **Margin** is `100 − second's percentage` and
   matches the second row; the tank's row has no trend arrow; its state follows the second one (Safe below 82 %,
   Warning from 82 %, Danger from 91 % or when somebody has more threat).
3. **Damage dealer's view**: your margin is `100 − your percentage`, in the header and in the tooltip.
4. **Healer / friend targeted**: target the tank, not the mob: the monitor shows the tank's enemy (the name changes
   with the tank's target). Turn *Follow the target of a friend* off (Settings, Monitor card): *No target*. Turn it on.
5. **Closing on the tank** (exit criterion): the tank holds back, the damage dealer builds threat. The row goes
   **Safe → Warning → Danger**, with the arrow up and `~N s` before the thresholds; write down the percentage at each
   change, the time between Danger and pulling aggro, and the percentage when the aggro actually changes (the monitor
   assumes 100 %).
6. **Back**: the damage dealer stops and the tank builds again. The states go back **without flicker**: at most one
   change a second for a row going down, no Warning/Safe jumping around 82 % or 91 % (hold about 80–84 % and 89–93 %
   for a minute and count the changes).
7. **Losing the mob**: the damage dealer pulls it. Header and shield move to the damage dealer, the tank's row says
   Danger with *Lost aggro*. The tank **taunts**: back to normal within a second or two.
8. **Two tanks** (if you have them): swap the mob with a taunt. The one who gives it away must not show *Lost aggro*.
9. **Arrows and predictions**: do the arrows point the right way? Is `~N s` ever far off? Note examples.
10. A member **leaves the threat list** (feign death, threat drop) or **dies**: the row disappears, no ghost row, no
    error. The tank dies: the header names the new holder.
11. Compare **responsiveness**: how long after the game's own aggro warning (or the mob turning) does the monitor
    change? Is the footer age ever above 0.5 s?

## 4. Targets: changes, deaths, vanishing

In a fight with several mobs:

1. Switch target quickly between two mobs: name, rows and states change at once; no rows of the previous mob remain,
   no leftover arrows.
2. Kill the targeted mob: **Target died** and an empty list. Deselect the corpse: still sensible. Target the next one.
3. Make a mob **vanish** (it despawns, evades and resets, you run out of range, a target that turns friendly, a
   boss phase that removes it): **Target lost** (or the right view), no stale numbers shown as live.
4. Clear the target in combat (`Esc`): *Target lost* (the client may not distinguish it from a mob that vanished).
5. Leave combat: **Combat ended**, the last rows stay, all marked stale (Unknown, `?`), footer *Not in combat*, pill
   *Idle*. At the next fight they are gone.
6. A mob **nobody in the group holds** (it chases an NPC or someone outside): *(not in the list)* after the name, or
   *Unknown tank*; no wrong tank.
7. `/reload` in the middle of a fight: the monitor starts again by itself within a second or two.

## 5. Dungeon and raid: values, state only, unknown

Do steps 3.1, 3.5, 4.1 and 4.2 again inside a **dungeon** (trash and a boss), then in a **raid** (and a battleground
if you wish). For each context, write down the pill (*Live*, *State only*, *Unavailable*) and:

1. **Values readable**: percentages for everyone, or only for some (for example only yourself)? Rows with and without
   numbers can sit together, each shown its own way. Name the cases.
2. **State only**: no bars, `—` instead of numbers, no margin or trend; each row still has icon and word; the tooltip
   says *State only: the client hides threat values here*; the footer starts with *State only*. For the tank holding
   the mob, the margin is a word (*Comfortable*, *Close*, *Very close*) or `—`.
3. **Unavailable**: one line, **Threat data unavailable** with its reason (*Secret value*, *Not available*, …). Never a
   row full of zeros.
4. **Unknown rows**: dashed bar, `?`, the reason in the tooltip, and *Stale* when old. Unknown rows are always last.
5. Hidden names show as *Party 2*, *Raid 13* or *Pet*, never empty; hidden roles show no icon.
6. Switch enemy nameplates on and off and use a **boss**: anything different?
7. No "action blocked" or "interface action failed", your action bars keep working.
8. `/atw debug` during a fight in every context; copy it each time.

## 6. The list: sorting, pin, narrow window

1. Click **Sort**: *Percentage*, *Absolute threat* (says *Needs threat values* where there are none), *Risk*, *Recent
   change*, and back. In each, check the order against the numbers. Unknown rows stay last.
2. The order changes at most once a second and **at once** when someone enters Danger; rows do not jump all the time.
3. **Me on top**: your row comes first; off again: it goes back to its place. Both choices survive `/reload`.
4. Shrink the window until not every row fits: the last line says **+N more** and your row stays visible. Do it in a
   raid too.
5. Narrow the window: margin column, role icon, trend and bar disappear in this order, but icon and word of the state
   stay; nothing overlaps.
6. **Readability in combat**: read the monitor while fighting, at window scale 50 %, 100 % and 200 %, the smallest and
   largest UI scale, in all three themes and in Italian. Note anything too small, cut, hard to tell apart (including
   class colours on Aurora) or distracting.

## 7. Demo mode

Out of combat, with no enemy targeted.

1. `/atw demo`: chat says it started; pill **Demo**, footer *DEMO · invented fight, not the game*, tooltips say the
   values are invented, mob **Demo golem**, your row says *You*.
2. Watch one turn (40 s): Warning with a prediction, Danger, the rogue taking the mob, the tank taking it back. No
   flicker, no error. It starts again by itself.
3. `/atw demo stop`, `/atw demo off`, `/atw demo` typed again and closing the window each end it.
4. **Real combat stops it**: during the demo, attack a mob: *Demo stopped: combat started* and the real monitor takes
   over at once, with no invented value left. In combat `/atw demo` answers that it cannot start.
5. `/reload` during the demo: it does not come back. Settings and window place are as you left them.
6. Change thresholds and rate while it runs (step 8): they apply at once.

## 8. Settings: the Monitor card

`/atw settings`, card **Monitor** (use the demo to see the effects).

1. **Warning** 50–95 %, **Danger** 60–100 %: Danger stays at least 5 points above Warning (moving one pushes the
   other); the bar ticks and states follow at once.
2. **Reads per second** 2–10 Hz (default 5): in a fight, `/atw debug` *Scheduler* shows `threat: N Hz (combat)`.
3. **Units per read** 5–40: set 5 in a group larger than that. You, your pet, the tanks and the holder update every
   time, the others in turn; are the older rows marked *Stale* too often? `/atw debug` *Threat scanner* shows the turns.
4. **Order of the list**, **Pin me on top** and **Follow the target of a friend** match the buttons of the list and
   survive `/reload`.
5. **When the window shows**:
   - *In group or combat* (default): closed window, solo: it opens when a fight starts and closes about 10 s after it
     (unless you reopened it or changed tab); closing it during a fight keeps it closed until the next; joining a group
     opens it and it stays; at login it is as you left it (open in a group);
   - *Always*: shown at every login and `/reload`, closes only by hand;
   - *Manually*: stays as you leave it.
6. **Reset settings** puts the monitor back to its defaults; **Start the demo** / **Stop the demo** work.

## 9. Interface in combat

During a fight: show, hide, move, resize, lock, switch tabs, change theme and monitor settings, open `/atw debug`. No
Lua error, no blocked action, no stutter on opening the window.

## 10. CPU and frame rate (40-player raid)

Best in a raid of 40 in a long fight (a boss or a training target). With fewer players, do it anyway and say how many.

1. Same place, same fight, `/atwp watch off`, no other change between measures. For each rate **2, 5 and 10 Hz** (Units
   per read 40), for at least a minute each:
   - the frame rate (`Ctrl+R` or the game's counter) with the monitor window **closed** and then **open** on the
     Monitor tab;
   - `/atw debug` **during the fight, window open**: the *Scheduler* line `threat: …, N ticks, X ms median, Y ms max`,
     the *Memory* lines and the *Threat scanner* lines.
2. Does the game stutter, especially when many events arrive (area damage, several pulls)? Does memory keep growing
   over a long fight (run `/atw debug` at the start and after 10 minutes)?
3. Repeat 5 Hz with **Units per read 10**.
4. With the window closed, *Scheduler* says `no tick running` and *Threat scanner* `idle` (nothing is read when nobody
   looks); out of combat too.
5. The offline estimate for 40 units is about 2 ms per read on a development PC (about 4, 10 and 20 ms per second at 2,
   5 and 10 Hz); the proposed budget of P10 is 1 ms per read and 5 ms per second. Your numbers decide the default rate
   and units ([SPEC-09 Q14](../specs/SPEC-09-risks-open-questions.md)).

## 11. Tuning notes (thresholds, Q7)

The owner decided the defaults on 2026-11-26 from play: **Warning 82 %** (ease off: fewer casts, lower ranks),
**Danger 91 %** (stop damage or healing for a moment); 3 points of hysteresis, 1 s before a state goes down, a predicted
Warning 3 s ahead ([SPEC-09 Q7](../specs/SPEC-09-risks-open-questions.md); they were 80 % and 95 %, provisional). A
saved pair still at 80 % and 95 % becomes 82 % and 91 % once; check that thresholds you set yourself stay as they were.

## What to report if something does not work

| Symptom | What to send |
|---|---|
| Lua error | the error text, what you did, `/atw debug` |
| Wrong or missing value, wrong tank, wrong state | context, a screenshot (names hidden), the row's tooltip, `/atw debug` during the fight, and the probe's `/atwp export` of the same session |
| *State only* or *Unavailable* where you expected numbers | context, `/atw debug` during the fight |
| Flicker or lag | the series of percentages and states you saw, rate, `/atw debug` |
| Slowness or stutter | step 10 numbers, group size, other addons |
